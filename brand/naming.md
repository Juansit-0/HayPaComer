# Brand Naming

## Naming strategy

**Brand in one line:** The measured kitchen assistant that decides tonight's dinner from what is actually at home.

**Audience:** Shared and family households in urban Latin America that cook at home and hate wasting food they paid for.

**Positioning angle:** Measured, honest, household-aware: grams instead of guesses, rules instead of promises.

**Emotional territory:** Calm confidence at dinner time; warmth of a real kitchen; fairness of a shared home.

**Naming directions explored:** (A) colloquial kitchen Spanish, (B) measurement language, (C) fair-share and shared table, (D) fridge warmth.

**Words to explore:** hay, comer, gramo, pesa, alcanza, cupo, ronda, nevera, medido.

**Words to avoid:** generic tech coinages, AI-suffix names, health-wellness vocabulary, English SaaS patterns.

## Top 5 names

1. **HayPaComer** - The brand's own answer in kitchen Spanish: "there is food to eat". Warm, memorable, and already the working name.
2. **Gramario** - A coined "inventory of grams" (gramo + the -ario suffix of recetario), measurement as the hero. Strong differentiation; some English speakers may hear "grammar".
3. **Alcanza** - "It is enough": the exact answer the product gives. Verb-first and emotional; an existing nonprofit uses the name.
4. **La Ronda** - The shared round of food; hospitality and fairness. Beautiful and warm, but less literal about measurement.
5. **Ya Hay** - "It is already there": the relief of discovering you do not need to shop. Short and colloquial; weaker as a mark.

## Evaluation of the working name: HayPaComer

**What it suggests:** Kitchen Spanish at its warmest: "hay pa' comer" means "there is food to eat". It suggests relief, abundance, and home rather than technology.

**What it makes people feel:** Appetite and closeness; it sounds like something a family member would say, not something a startup would invent.

**Category fit:** Strong. It is unmistakably food and kitchen, and it answers the exact nightly question without explaining anything.

**Premium or cheap feel:** Accessible and warm, exactly matching the positioning. It never reads premium, which is a feature here.

**Easy to say:** Yes in Spanish. English speakers can pronounce it after one hearing ("eye-pah-ko-MER"), though the "Hay" spelling can initially read as the English word "hay".

**Easy to spell:** Yes in Spanish; apostrophes are avoided, which keeps it typeable. Non-Spanish speakers may need one repetition.

**Logo potential:** Strong. The three beats (Hay / Pa' / Comer) allow a stacked wordmark, a "pa'" apostrophe as a graphic device, and a cutlery or tray mark built from the "H".

**Domain and trademark risk:** The phrase is generic colloquial Spanish, so trademark scope is limited but conflict is unlikely; domain variants are the main check (haypacomer.com, haypacomer.app).

**Fits the audience:** Yes. It speaks the audience's own words, which is the whole brand voice in one name.

**Score: 9/10**

## Recommendation

**Keep HayPaComer and refine it into the final name.** The name is the brand's strategy in miniature: it is the answer ("there is food to eat"), not the mechanism. Measurement is expressed by the tagline and identity (grams, scale, numbers), where it can be shown rather than encoded. Switching to Gramario would sharpen the measurement story but trade away warmth, introduce a "grammar" search risk in English, and ask the audience to learn a coined word.

Refinements to lock in the brand book:

- Wordmark form: **HayPaComer** in running text; optionally **Hay Pa' Comer** as a display lockup for campaign use, with the apostrophe as a design accent.
- Pronunciation guide for English speakers: "eye-pah-ko-MER (there is food to eat)".
- Always lowercase the story in copy: "hay pa' comer", the phrase; capitalized as the proper name.
- Pair with the tagline "Weigh it. Know it. Cook it." so the measurement promise travels with the name.

If the team decides that category-defining measurement matters more than warmth, **Gramario** is the strongest alternative, with **Alcanza** as the emotional dark horse.

## Before you finalize

- [ ] Check domain availability (haypacomer.com, haypacomer.app)

- [ ] Check social handles availability
- [ ] Say it out loud in Spanish and English: does it feel natural?
- [ ] Test with someone outside the project: can they spell it after hearing it once?
- [ ] Check unintended meanings in target market languages (Spanish, Portuguese, English)

> Naming strategy and shortlists are creative guidance, not legal advice. Verify domain, trademark, and handle availability before locking the name.
