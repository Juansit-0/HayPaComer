# Brand Voice Guide

## 01 Voice overview

HayPaComer talks like the most organized person in a shared apartment: calm, practical, and good with numbers, without ever making anyone feel judged. It speaks in grams, dates, and minutes because that is what a weeknight needs, and it treats the kitchen as a shared space with real people and real budgets. The voice is warm in its vocabulary and rigorous in its facts: it explains rules when safety is involved and admits what it does not know. It never hypes, never scolds, and never hides a failure behind cheerful language. Every sentence should help someone decide, cook, or trust.

**Voice essence:** HayPaComer sounds like a well-organized roommate who is great with numbers - direct, warm, and honest.

## 02 Tone dimensions

**Formality - casual side, but not sloppy.**
Practical everyday language with correct structure; contractions welcome, slang kept minimal and only where it travels across Spanish-speaking markets.
Example: "Weigh the tray, then confirm. Stock updates in 192 g."

**Energy - calm.**
No urgency theater. Alerts are informative and specific, not alarming. Energy comes from clarity, not from punctuation.
Example: "Door open for 40 seconds. Check the dairy zone."

**Humor - dry and occasional, never about waste or safety.**
A light observation is allowed when nothing is at risk. Food safety and someone's wasted money are never jokes.
Example: "The soup has been waiting since Tuesday. Tonight is its night."

**Expertise - accessible authority.**
The system knows its rules and states them plainly, without lecturing. When a decision comes from a rule, the rule is named.
Example: "Under review: 6 hours above 8 degrees. The cold rule says check before eating."

**Warmth - intimate but respectful.**
It speaks to a household, uses names when they exist, and respects that food has owners. It never uses guilt as motivation.
Example: "Ana's cheese stays Ana's. Ask before you cook it."

## 03 Voice qualities

**Direct**
HayPaComer leads with the action or the number, not with context. The first words answer the question that was asked.
**Do:** "Cook the spinach tonight." **Don't:** "You might want to consider using the spinach soon."

**Concrete**
Adjectives are replaced by facts whenever a fact exists. If a number is unknown, the copy says what is known and what is missing.
**Do:** "200 g needed, 80 g weighed." **Don't:** "Looks like you might be a bit short."

**Warm**
It talks like a person sharing a kitchen, not like software addressing a user. Names, households, and first person plural are natural here.
**Do:** "Your household rescued 2.3 kg this month." **Don't:** "User waste reduction: 2.3 kg."

**Honest about limits**
The product says "under review" when the cold chain is uncertain and "the rules say" when safety is at stake. Admitting uncertainty builds more trust than pretending.
**Do:** "Not enough data to judge. Check it yourself before eating." **Don't:** "Probably fine!"

**Calm in failure**
Outages and errors are explained in one line with what still works. No drama, no apologies longer than the problem.
**Do:** "AI is offline. Rules still suggest dinner." **Don't:** "We are so sorry for the inconvenience!"

**Respectful of ownership**
Permissions and fairness are stated as facts, never as warnings. Sharing a fridge is a social contract, and the copy honors it.
**Do:** "Yours, shared, or ask first." **Don't:** "Warning: unauthorized food access detected."

## 04 Vocabulary

**Words we use:** weigh, grams, tray, stock, zone, expires, rescue, under review, enough, reduce, substitute, portion, tonight, minutes, household, roommates, share, confirm, rules, measured, saved.

**Words we avoid:** revolutionize, magic, seamless, effortless, empower, leverage, solution, platform, smart kitchen, AI-powered (as a selling promise), food waste warrior, guilt, should have, oops, hurry, limited time, world-class, delight.

**Brand-specific language:**

- People are a **household**, **roommates**, or **family**; "users" only appears in technical documentation.
- The fridge holds **stock** organized in **zones** and **trays**.
- Product states have fixed names: **cook now**, **rescue first**, **under review**, **degraded mode**.
- Measurement verbs stay literal: we **weigh**, **compare**, **discount**, **rescale**; we never "detect" what we did not measure.

## 05 Writing style rules

**Sentence length:** Short to medium, one idea per sentence. Default under 20 words; longer sentences only in documentation prose.

**Punctuation:** No exclamation marks in UI or marketing; at most one per page in social copy. Em dashes are rare and only for asides. No ellipses in product copy. Question marks belong to real questions.

**Capitalization:** Sentence case everywhere, including buttons and titles. The brand is always HayPaComer; the phrase behind it is written lowercase: "hay pa' comer".

**Numbers:** Numerals for every measurement and quantity, always with units: 200 g, 15 min, 2 people, 192 g used. Spell out numbers only when they open a sentence or when no unit applies ("Two items expire today").

**Contractions:** Yes, they keep the voice human: "it's", "you're", "don't".

**Active vs passive:** Active by default: "Java validates the grams", not "the grams are validated". Passive is acceptable in architecture and protocol documentation.

**Humor:** Allowed sparingly, dry, and never at the expense of wasted food, money, or safety.

## 06 Channel adaptations

| Channel | Tone adjustment | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website homepage | Calm and concrete; one number as proof | "200 g needed, 80 g home. Reduce or substitute." |
| Social media | Same voice, sharper opening; still no hype | "The soup has been waiting since Tuesday." |
| Email or notifications | Shortest possible; lead with the fact | "Door open 40 s. Dairy zone, check the milk." |
| Customer support | Warmer, slower, still direct; confirm what was done | "Fixed. Your stock is back to 650 g." |
| Error messages and UI copy | One line: what happened, what still works | "AI is offline. Rules still suggest dinner." |

## 07 Before and after examples

**Before (off-brand):**
> "Oops! You have 3 items expiring soon. Don't let them go to waste!"

**After (on-brand):**
> "3 items expire this week. Soup first, then the spinach."

**Why it works:** Removes the guilt and the exclamation; gives an order of action.

**Before (off-brand):**
> "Revolutionize your kitchen with AI-powered inventory magic."

**After (on-brand):**
> "Weigh the tray. Know your stock. Cook what expires first."

**Why it works:** Replaces hype with the actual product loop.

**Before (off-brand):**
> "Congratulations! You saved the planet by rescuing 2.3 kg of food!"

**After (on-brand):**
> "2.3 kg rescued this month. About 45,000 pesos not thrown away."

**Why it works:** Converts vague praise into a measured, local result.

**Before (off-brand):**
> "Error 500: Internal server error. We apologize for any inconvenience."

**After (on-brand):**
> "Something failed on our side. Inventory and alerts still work. Try again in a moment."

**Why it works:** States the failure, then what still works.

**Before (off-brand):**
> "Are you sure you want to delete this item? This action cannot be undone!"

**After (on-brand):**
> "Delete the yogurt? This cannot be undone."

**Why it works:** Same warning, zero drama, fewer words.

## 08 Things we never say

- Hollow hype: world-class, revolutionary, game-changing, next-level, seamless, magic.
- "AI-powered" as a headline benefit; AI is a helper, rules are the truth.
- Guilt or blame about waste: "you should have", "how could you forget".
- False urgency: "hurry", "only today", "last chance".
- Wellness scoring and health promises the product cannot verify.
- Sarcasm, passive-aggressive humor, or teasing about someone's food.
- Corporate hedging: "we regret to inform", "it has come to our attention".
- "User" in customer-facing copy; people are a household, roommates, or family.
