# Target Audience

## 01 Audience overview

Shared and family households in urban Latin America that cook at home several times a week: roommates pooling groceries in one fridge, and young parents juggling work, kids, and dinner. They are not foodies or gadget collectors; they are people trying to feed a household without wasting money or food. Current options fail them: recipe apps assume a perfectly stocked pantry, premium smart fridges are out of reach, and notes plus memory leave blind spots. They want one honest answer: what can I cook now with what is actually here.

## 02 Primary audience profile

**Demographics**

- Age range: 20 to 40.
- Household shape: 2 to 5 people sharing one fridge (roommates) or a young family with small children.
- Location: urban Latin America (apartments); a secondary audience is shared households in other regions.
- Income: students with limited budgets, early-career professionals, and young families with entry to middle income.
- Life stage: university years, first jobs and shared rent, first years of parenthood.

**Psychographics**

- Core values: saving money, not wasting food, fair sharing among roommates, practical technology.
- Aspirations: calm weeknights, groceries that actually get used, less guilt about thrown-away food.
- Self-identity: practical and organized enough, conscious about waste, not a kitchen perfectionist.
- Brands they already love and why: WhatsApp (coordination), Rappi-style delivery (convenience), Xiaomi-style smart home gear (value), budget recipe creators (honest, simple).
- Content they consume: cooking shorts, budget and meal-prep tips, apartment-life content, honest tech reviews.
- Communities: university groups, roommate WhatsApp groups, frugal-cooking and home-cooking groups.

**Behavioral patterns**

- Purchase decisions: value-driven and word-of-mouth first; small devices are quick purchases, anything expensive needs proof.
- Triggers: spoiled groceries, a "there is nothing to eat" dinner, someone eating food that was not theirs, a power outage, moving into a new apartment.
- Discovery: short-form video, WhatsApp recommendations, university maker and tech communities.
- Buying process: wants to see it work (demo or video) before paying; skeptical of promises without measurable results.

## 03 Audience personas

### Mateo, 22 - computer science student sharing an apartment, Bogota

> "I buy groceries on Sunday and by Wednesday I have no idea what is still in there."

**Their World:** Mateo shares a fridge with two roommates. Everyone buys food, nobody labels it, and Tupperware becomes archaeological layers. He cooks simple meals between classes and projects, and hates throwing away food he paid for.

**Goals:**

- Cook something decent with what is already home instead of ordering delivery again.
- Stop buying duplicates because nobody knows what is in the back.
- Keep roommates accountable without awkward conversations about whose food is whose.

**Frustrations:**

- Recipe apps assume ingredients he does not have and never account for grams.
- Shared fridge means his food disappears or expires without warning.
- Paper notes on the fridge door last one day.

**What they need from a brand like this:** Something cheap, quick, and honest that measures what is really in the fridge and respects who owns what. No subscriptions for basics.

**Decision triggers:** Buys after seeing a live demo with grams on a scale; hesitates if hardware looks expensive or hard to install.

**Channels they are on:** TikTok, YouTube, Discord and university groups, WhatsApp.

**How HayPaComer solves their problem:** The scale and inventory turn "I think there is chicken" into "you have 200 g of chicken and it expires tomorrow", so Mateo cooks instead of ordering.

### Ana, 34 - operations coordinator and mother of two, Medellin

> "I plan dinner at 6 p.m. with two kids asking for food and no idea what is left in the fridge."

**Their World:** Ana runs a household with her partner and two small children. She does the big grocery run, juggles leftovers, and carries the mental load of feeding everyone. Food waste feels like personal failure and lost money.

**Goals:**

- Decide dinner in minutes with what is already home.
- Use leftovers safely and without repeating the same dish all week.
- Cut the grocery bill by avoiding duplicates and waste.

**Frustrations:**

- No trustworthy view of quantities and expiry dates.
- Safety doubts after a power outage or a long-open door.
- Meal planning apps demand time she does not have.

**What they need from a brand like this:** A helper that answers with evidence (grams, dates, rules), never invents safety, and works even when the internet fails.

**Decision triggers:** Buys when she sees waste avoided in pesos and kg; hesitates if setup takes an afternoon or if the family must change all its habits at once.

**Channels they are on:** Instagram, WhatsApp family groups, YouTube cooking channels, Facebook communities.

**How HayPaComer solves their problem:** Cold-chain rules and expiry alerts protect the family, and rescue-first suggestions turn leftovers into tonight's dinner.

### Camila, 27 - junior designer sharing a flat, Mexico City

> "I want to eat well on a budget, but I am not going to keep a spreadsheet for my fridge."

**Their World:** Camila lives with a roommate and eats at home most nights. She cares about waste and money but will abandon any tool that adds friction. She coordinates groceries with her roommate over chat.

**Goals:**

- Eat home-cooked meals on weeknights without planning ahead.
- Share the shopping list without duplicating purchases.
- Reduce what she throws away each week.

**Frustrations:**

- Manual inventory apps die after a week of updates.
- "Who bought this?" fights over shared food.
- Recipe suggestions that ignore what is about to expire.

**What they need from a brand like this:** Almost zero-effort registration (weigh it, confirm it), a live shared list, and suggestions that use what is about to expire first.

**Decision triggers:** Buys when the weighing flow takes seconds; hesitates with any mandatory daily logging ritual.

**Channels they are on:** Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, roommate group chat.

**How HayPaComer solves their problem:** The fridge-mode tray updates stock by weighing, and the collaborative list keeps both roommates in sync without duplicates.

## 04 Audience language

**Their problem:**

- "I never know what is actually in the fridge."
- "I bought onions and there were already three bags at home."
- "That is my roommate's cheese, nobody touches it."
- "The power went out, is this still good?"
- "I have 15 minutes and no idea what to cook."

**Their desired outcome:**

- "Dinner decided in five minutes."
- "Nothing ended up in the trash this week."
- "Everyone sees the same list."
- "It tells me exactly how much is left, in grams."

**Why they choose alternatives today:**

- "Recipe apps assume I already have everything."
- "Those smart fridges cost a fortune."
- "I just use my notes app and memory."

**Brand-building implication:** Lead with the plain questions people already ask, answer with measured facts (grams, dates, minutes), and never overpromise safety. The language is concrete, short, and honest.

## 05 Who this brand is NOT for

- Premium smart-fridge buyers: they decide on status and ecosystem, not on a cheap instrumented tray.
- Delivery-first households that never cook: there is no inventory flow to manage.
- Professional kitchens and restaurants: different scale, regulation, and reliability requirements.

## 06 Audience implications

**Messaging implications:** Lead with the nightly question ("what can I cook right now?") and proof (grams, expiry, minutes). Avoid abstract claims like "smart kitchen platform".

**Channel implications:** Short demos on TikTok and Instagram, honest build logs on YouTube, and WhatsApp-friendly artifacts (shareable list, notifications) inside the product itself.

**Voice implications:** Practical, approachable, honest, and curious; short sentences; verbs first; no paternalism, no false guarantees.

**Product and experience implications:** Weighing must take seconds, suggestions must prioritize expiring items, ownership and permissions must be visible, and everything essential must work offline.
